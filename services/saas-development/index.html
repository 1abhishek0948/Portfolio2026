SaaS Development Services

I build SaaS products for founders who need a working product, not a slide deck. Authentication, user roles, dashboards, APIs and a solid data layer — the unglamorous parts done right, so your product can actually grow.

What a SaaS build includes

  • Accounts and authentication — sign-up, login, password reset, session and JWT handling done securely
  • Roles and permissions — admins, members, and whatever distinctions your product needs
  • Core product features — the actual thing your users pay for, scoped into a realistic first version
  • Dashboards and reporting — usage data, admin views and the numbers you need to run the product
  • REST APIs — a clean interface layer so future mobile apps or integrations are straightforward
  • Deployment and operations — environments, monitoring basics and a repeatable release process

MVP first, scale later

The right first version is smaller than you think. I'll push for an MVP that proves the core value with real users — then we evolve the architecture based on evidence, not guesses. My stack choices (Django, PostgreSQL, React) are deliberately boring and proven: they scale further than most products ever need, without exotic complexity. For examples of multi-user product work, see Lafzloom or the AI-powered projects section.

What I don't do

Honest limits: I don't run your ads, I don't guarantee your growth, and I'm not the right hire for a low-code experiment. If your idea needs a team of ten on day one, I'll say so. What I do give you is a real, working product and a developer who answers email — which is rarer than it should be.

Where I work

Founders in Kathmandu and across Nepal work with me remotely; international founders too. Time zones are handled, not ignored.

Frequently asked questions

What's a realistic MVP timeline?

A focused MVP is typically measured in weeks to a few months depending on scope. After a scoping call you'll get an estimate with phases, so you can see what lands when.

Which stack do you use for SaaS?

Django and PostgreSQL on the backend, React on the frontend, with JWT authentication and REST APIs. It's the stack behind my own products and it's maintainable by any competent team you hire later.

Can you add billing and subscriptions?

Yes — I integrate payment gateways and subscription logic when the product is ready for it. I usually recommend proving the core product first; premature billing code is wasted if the product changes.

Can you take over an existing SaaS codebase?

Possibly — it depends on the stack and the state of the code. Send me what you have (repo access or a description) and I'll give you an honest assessment before you commit.
