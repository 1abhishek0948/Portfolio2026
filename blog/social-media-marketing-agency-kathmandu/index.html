Social Media Marketing Agency in Kathmandu: What to Expect and How to Pick

What a social media marketing agency in Kathmandu should deliver, how to judge a proposal, and the specific signals that separate an agency that builds an audience from one that just posts.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 4 min read

"Social media marketing agency in Kathmandu" is one of the most searched phrases in Nepal, which tells you two things: a lot of people need it, and a lot of people are choosing wrong. The difference between an agency that builds an audience you can sell into and one that just produces posts is measurable, and it is in the goal, the plan and the metric, not in the visuals. This guide is what to expect, and the signals that separate the two.

Platform norms and costs change, and each agency's fees differ, so confirm specifics with the one you are considering. This is a how-to-judge guide, not a recommendation of any firm.

What a social media agency should deliver

Strip it to four things, and you can judge any proposal against them:

Deliverable What it means The failure mode
A defined goal A customer-level aim, not "more followers" Posting without a destination
A platform plan What each platform is for, and why One content style pasted everywhere
A cadence and owner A schedule and a named person Content that stalls when the ad stops
A metric you can read A number tied to the goal Reporting only impressions

The last one is the tell. An agency that reports impressions is telling you it ran; an agency that reports a customer-level result is telling you it worked.

The goal first, and why it beats the visuals

The most common mistake is hiring for the visuals. But a beautiful post that has no goal is decoration. The order is:

  1. The goal, a customer-level number: a lead, a sale, a booking, a message.
  2. The platforms that actually reach that customer in your market, not the ones the agency likes.
  3. The content that fits each platform, which is where the visuals earn their place.
  4. The metric that tells you if the goal is moving, read against the cost.

A proposal that leads with the visuals and has no goal and no metric is a design pitch. It will look great and produce nothing you can act on. Keep that in mind when you read the deck.

The specific signals that separate the good from the busy

When you compare two or three Kathmandu social agencies, lean on the hard-to-fake signals:

  • A customer-level metric, in writing. Not "growth," a number.
  • A named owner for your account, not "the team."
  • A plan for the pause. What holds up when the paid push stops, because that is where the real audience lives.
  • A reference you can call, from a client in a market like yours.
  • A scope that says what is out, which is the line vague proposals omit.

These five are the same instincts as evaluating any marketing firm; see how to evaluate a digital marketing company in Nepal for the full method, and what an agency does for the four jobs.

The metrics to ask for, and the one to avoid

Ask for a small set that maps to the goal:

  • Reach and engagement to confirm the content is seen and acted on.
  • A customer-level result, the lead, sale or booking, and its source.
  • The cost against it, so you know if the customer is worth it.

Avoid follower count as the target. It is slow, easy to game, and tells you nothing about a customer. An agency that chases followers is optimizing the wrong number, and you will be paying for it.

A short proposal review you can do in ten minutes

Run the proposal past this list before you pay:

  • [ ] A customer-level goal, stated as a number
  • [ ] A platform-by-platform plan, not one style everywhere
  • [ ] A content cadence and a named owner
  • [ ] A metric you can read, tied to the goal
  • [ ] The cost shape, and what is in and out of the scope
  • [ ] A reference in a market like yours

If a proposal clears all six, it is a marketing plan. If it clears only the visuals, it is a design pitch and it will not move a customer.

For the price side of the decision, the spend has three layers and a cost per customer to track, covered in digital marketing services price in Nepal.

Bottom line: a social media agency in Kathmandu is good when it has a goal, a plan per platform, a named owner, and a customer-level metric, and it will tell you what happens when the ad stops. Judge the proposal against those, not against the visuals, and you will hire the agency that builds an audience you can sell into, not the one that just posts.

FAQ

What should a social media agency actually deliver?

A consistent, on-brand presence, content that fits each platform, the measurement to know if it is working, and a plan for turning attention into a customer. Posts without the last two are decoration, not marketing.

How do I judge a social agency's proposal?

Look for a defined goal, a platform-by-platform plan, a content cadence, and a customer-level metric. A proposal that is all visuals and no goal or number is a design pitch, not a marketing plan.

Is a follower count the right target?

No. Followers are a lagging, gameable number. The useful targets are a customer-level result (a lead, a sale, a booking) and a cost against it.
