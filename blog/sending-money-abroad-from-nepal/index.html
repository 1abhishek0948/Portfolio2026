Sending Money Abroad from Nepal: Methods and What to Check

How to send money abroad from Nepal: the main methods, the limits that apply, the fees to compare, and the documents that keep the transfer clean.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 2 min read

Sending money out of Nepal is the mirror of receiving it, and it is where the regulated side shows up most: you are moving foreign currency, and that is a controlled flow. This guide is the method, the limit, the fee and the record, in the order that keeps the transfer clean.

Limits, allowed purposes and documents are set by the regulator and the bank and they change, so confirm the current position at your branch. This is general information, not financial advice.

The two lanes

Method Best for What to confirm
Your bank A documented need, a larger sum The limit, the purpose document, the cost
Licensed remittance operator A personal transfer, speed The fee, the corridor, the landing

The honest split: the bank is the clean lane for a transfer that has a paper reason; the operator is the fast lane for a personal one. Picking the lane is step one.

The limit is the whole game

Because this is foreign currency leaving the country, the amount you can move and the paperwork that goes with it are set, and they change. The move that saves everyone is to confirm two things at your branch before you plan: the current ceiling for your situation, and the documents it needs. Do not size the transfer from memory; size it from the branch's current answer. The shape of those rules is in NRB foreign currency rules.

The fee, compared honestly

Compare the all-in cost, not the sticker: the transfer fee, any intermediary charge, and the exchange-rate spread between your rate and the mid-market. Two lanes that look the same on the fee can be very different on the spread, and the spread is where a lot of the money quietly goes.

The record that keeps it clean

One dated line on your side: the purpose, the amount, the channel, the date, and the document it required. That is the proof that the transfer had a reason, and it is the thing you would hand over if the question ever came.

A sensible default

Pick the lane that matches the size and the reason, confirm the current limit and documents at the branch, compare the all-in cost on the spread not the sticker, and write the one dated line. Do that and the transfer is clean on the side that sent it, which is the side that has to explain it.

Bottom line: sending money abroad from Nepal is a controlled flow, so it is about the limit, the lane and the record. Confirm the ceiling and the document at the branch, compare the spread not the fee, and keep one dated line, and the transfer stays clean.

FAQ

What are the main ways to send money abroad from Nepal?

Through your bank or a licensed remittance operator, depending on the amount and the destination. The bank is the cleanest for a documented need, and a licensed operator is often faster for a personal transfer. The choice depends on what you are sending and why.

Is there a limit on how much I can send?

Yes, foreign-currency movement is regulated, and the ceiling and the paperwork depend on the amount and the reason. Confirm the current limit and the documents it requires before you plan the transfer, because that is the part that changes.

What should I keep for the record?

The purpose, the amount, the channel and the date, and whatever document the transfer required. One dated line that says why the money went is what keeps the transfer clean on your side.

Sources and how to verify

Foreign-currency ceilings, card terms and bank fees are set by the NRB and each bank and change; confirm the current limit and fee with your bank before you rely on a number. General information, not financial advice.
