Building an Accessible Website: A Practical Checklist

The accessible website, without the jargon: what it actually means for a small business, the few checks that cover most of it, and why it is the cheap version of the professional one.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 3 min read

The accessible site is not the one that passes the audit; it is the one that works for the visitor with the slow connection, the small screen, or the screen reader, and for Nepal those are not the edge cases, they are the main ones. This guide is the practical version, the few checks that cover most of it, and why it is the cheap side of the professional one.

This is a checklist, not a certification; it covers the common cases, not the full standard.

The three checks that cover most of it

Check What it is Why it is the cheap win
The contrast that is readable The text is readable on the background, for the eye that is not perfect It is a number, not a redesign, and it helps everyone
The text that has an alternative The image has the words, and the video has the caption It is the caption and the alt, which is the cheap version of the content
The focus that a keyboard can reach The visitor who does not use a mouse can still move through the page It is the tab order, which is the fix that the mouse user never notices

The three are the most, and they are the cheap ones, because they are a number, a caption and an order, not a rebuild, and that is why they are the version a small site can actually do.

Why it is the cheap version of the professional one

The professional audit is the full standard, and it is the right thing for the large site, and the small site does not need the full standard to be the accessible one, it needs the three, which are the version that helps the real visitor, the one with the mid-tier connection and the small screen and the screen reader, and that is the visitor in Nepal, not the one the standard imagines. The three are the accessibility that is the site working for more of the people who use it, and that is the definition, not the certificate.

The habit that keeps it, the check on the change

The accessibility is not the launch, it is the check on every change, because the new feature is where it breaks, and the page that was accessible is the one that the new button made it not. The habit is the check on the change, the contrast and the alt and the focus on the new piece, and that is why it is a practice, and the practice is the cheap one, because it is the check, not the project. The site that is checked on the change is the one that stays the accessible one, and the one that is not is the one that quietly stops being it.

The connection to the other habits

The accessible site is the mobile-first one, because the small screen and the slow connection are the same visitor, and the check is the same, which is why the two are one habit, covered in mobile-first design for low-bandwidth Nepal, and it is the one that makes the landing page the one that works for the visitor, in landing pages that convert Nepali visitors.

A sensible default

Do the three, the contrast, the alt and the focus, and check them on every change. The three are the cheap version of the professional audit, and they are the site that works for the real Nepali visitor, and that is the accessible one, by the definition that matters.

Bottom line: the accessible site is the one that works for the slow connection, the small screen and the screen reader, and the three checks, the contrast, the alt and the focus, are the cheap version that covers most of it. Do the three and check them on the change, and the site is the accessible one for the visitor who actually uses it.

FAQ

Does a small business site really need accessibility?

The useful version, yes, because the accessible site is the one that works for the person with a slow connection, a small screen, or a screen reader, and those are not niche users in Nepal, they are the real ones. The accessibility is the site that works for more of your actual visitors.

What is the cheapest way to get most of it?

The three: the contrast that is readable, the text that has an alternative, and the focus that a keyboard can reach. Those three cover the most, and they are the cheap ones, ahead of the full audit.

Is accessibility a one-time fix?

No, it is a check at every change, because the new feature is where it breaks. The habit is the check on the change, not the audit on the launch, which is why it is a practice, not a project.
