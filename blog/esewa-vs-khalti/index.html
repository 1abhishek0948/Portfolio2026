eSewa vs Khalti: Which Nepali Wallet Should You Use?

eSewa vs Khalti in Nepal: what each is good at, the real differences in features, fees and acceptance, and how to pick the one that fits how you pay and get paid.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 2 min read

People in Nepal usually have both, and the question is not "which is better" in the abstract. It is "which one is actually useful to me, for the payments I make and the money I take." eSewa and Khalti overlap a lot, but they differ in ways that matter day to day. This guide is the comparison that lets you pick with your own numbers.

Fees, limits, supported partners and features change, so treat the specifics below as a 2026 starting point and confirm the current ones in each app. This is general guidance, not financial advice.

What each one actually is

Both are registered digital wallets you top up and pay with. The meaningful differences:

Dimension eSewa Khalti
Top-up options Varies; check current cards/banks Varies; check current cards/banks
Typical strength Local utility, bills, some transfers Merchant acceptance, promos
International use Not for foreign SaaS Not for foreign SaaS
The real question What do you actually pay with it? What do you actually pay with it?

The last row is the point: the "best" wallet is the one matched to your real transactions, not the one with the most marketing.

How to pick on your own numbers

Run your money through the wallet you want to use and see what fits:

  • What do I pay most? List your top three recurring payments and whether each accepts eSewa, Khalti or neither. The wallet that covers two of the three wins for you.
  • How do I top up cleanly? A wallet you cannot reliably fund is useless. Confirm which cards or banks load it for you, at what cost.
  • Do I take money in? If you are selling to local customers, accept on the wallet your customers actually use, and note the settlement time and fee to your account.

For the developer side of taking those payments, see eSewa, Khalti and IME Pay gateway APIs.

The one thing neither is for

Neither eSewa nor Khalti is a cross-border tool. They are local. If your goal is paying a foreign host, subscription or ad platform, or moving foreign earnings home, you are in a different category: cards, a receiving platform or NRB-regulated foreign-currency accounts. See how to pay for foreign hosting from Nepal and how freelancers in Nepal get paid.

A sensible default

Start with the wallet your most frequent payment accepts. Keep the other one if a second common payment needs it. Neither is a replacement for the cross-border tools, so keep those separate and stop mixing the two jobs in your head.

Bottom line: pick on your own transactions, not the brand. Check what you actually pay, how you top up cleanly, and whether either covers a foreign payment (they do not). Do that and the "eSewa or Khalti" question answers itself.

FAQ

Can I use both eSewa and Khalti at the same time?

Yes. They are separate wallets and holding both costs nothing. Many people keep one for receiving and the other for paying, because acceptance and the top-up options differ between them.

Which one has more acceptance in Nepal?

Both are widely accepted for local digital payments. Acceptance of each specific partner or merchant differs, so the practical answer is to check which one the places you actually pay support, not which has the bigger brand.

Do these wallets work for paying foreign hosts or SaaS?

No. eSewa and Khalti are Nepal's local wallets, and most international hosting and SaaS vendors do not accept them. For foreign payments you need a card or a cross-border method, which is a different tool.

Sources and how to verify

Foreign-currency ceilings, card terms and bank fees are set by the NRB and each bank and change; confirm the current limit and fee with your bank before you rely on a number. General information, not financial advice.
