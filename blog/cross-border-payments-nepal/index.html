Cross-Border Payments from Nepal to the US and EU: What Works

The realistic ways to pay or move money between Nepal and the US or EU: cards, receiving platforms, transfer services and wires, with the honest limits of each.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 2 min read

Paying across a border from Nepal is not one problem; it is three tools doing three jobs, and the failure is always using the wrong tool for the job. This guide lines up the tools, the jobs, and the limit that sits underneath all of it.

Fees, supported corridors and NRB rules change, so confirm the current ones. This is general guidance, not financial advice.

The three tools, by job

Job The tool that fits What to check
Pay a foreign vendor once An international card That online/international is on, and the limit clears it
Pay a foreign vendor repeatedly A receiving/transfer platform the vendor accepts That the vendor actually accepts it, and the all-in cost
Move a known sum abroad A bank wire or transfer service The limit, the document, the spread

The card is the easy one. The platform is the recurring one. The wire is the documented one. Pick by the job, and the "which is best" question disappears.

The local wallets are not in this list

This is the mistake that costs the most time. eSewa and Khalti are for inside Nepal. They do not pay a US or EU vendor, and they are not a cross-border tool. If your plan is "I will use my local wallet for my foreign bill," that plan will not run; the money has to move through a card or a cross-border service instead. The full foreign-payment picture is in how to pay for foreign hosting from Nepal and PayPal alternatives in Nepal.

The limit underneath all of it

Every one of the three is moving foreign currency, which is a regulated flow in Nepal. The habit that keeps any of them clean is the same: confirm the current ceiling and the document it needs at your branch before the payment, and keep the record of the transaction. See NRB dollar card rules and limits for the shape of that.

A sensible default

For the one-off, use the card. For the recurring, find the platform the vendor accepts and price its spread. For a known sum, run the wire and keep the document. Keep the local wallets out of all three, and confirm the limit before the big ones.

Bottom line: cross-border payment from Nepal is a card for the one-off, a vendor-accepted platform for the recurring, and a documented wire for the known sum, with the NRB limit checked underneath. Match the tool to the job and the local wallets stay where they belong, at home.

FAQ

What is the cleanest way to pay a US or EU vendor from Nepal?

Usually an international card for a one-off payment, or a receiving/transfer platform that the vendor accepts for a recurring one. The cleanest is the one that matches the vendor's accepted methods, so check that before you build a workflow around a tool.

Can I use eSewa or Khalti to pay a foreign company?

No. Those are local Nepali wallets, and foreign SaaS, hosting and most EU/US vendors do not accept them. Cross-border payment is a card or a cross-border service, a different tool entirely.

Do NRB rules apply to this?

Yes, wherever you are moving foreign currency, the regulated side applies. Confirm the current ceiling and the documents it needs at your branch before you rely on a big cross-border payment.

Sources and how to verify

Foreign-currency ceilings, card terms and bank fees are set by the NRB and each bank and change; confirm the current limit and fee with your bank before you rely on a number. General information, not financial advice.
