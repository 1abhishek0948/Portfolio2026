IME Pay and eSewa IME: A Guide to Nepali Digital Wallets

What IME Pay and eSewa IME are, how Nepal's main digital wallets fit together, and how to use them for everyday local payments without overcomplicating it.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 2 min read

Nepal's local money runs on a handful of digital wallets, and the names get confused because some are related. This guide sorts out what IME Pay and eSewa IME are, how they relate, and the simple way to use them without carrying five apps you never use.

Specifics like fees, limits, top-up options and accepted merchants change, so confirm the current ones in each app. This is general guidance, not financial advice.

What each name means

The short version: eSewa is the long-established wallet, and IME Pay is its successor-era, broader digital-payments platform, with "eSewa IME" the bridge between the two. In practice, you will meet all three names and they are more connected than separate. The thing that matters to you is simpler:

  • An account and a balance you top up and pay from.
  • Top-up paths (which cards or banks load it, and at what cost).
  • Acceptance (which merchants, bills and transfers it covers).

How to use them without overcomplicating

You do not need all the names. You need one clean loop:

  1. Pick the one your most frequent payment accepts. That is your main wallet.
  2. Make sure you can top it up cleanly, with a card or bank you already have, at a known cost.
  3. Keep a second only where a payment forces it, not out of habit.

For taking local online payments as a business, the relevant question is which wallet your customers use and what the fee is; that is the subject of eSewa, Khalti and IME Pay gateway APIs for developers.

The boundary that matters

These wallets are for inside Nepal. They do not pay a foreign host or SaaS, and they are not the cleanest way to move foreign earnings home. For that, you are in the cross-border category: cards, a receiving platform, and NRB-regulated foreign-currency accounts. See how to pay for foreign hosting from Nepal and how freelancers in Nepal get paid.

A sensible default

Use one wallet for the payment you make most, fund it from a card or bank you already have, and stop there. Add a second wallet only when a specific payment requires it. Keep the cross-border tools separate, because they are a different job.

Bottom line: treat IME Pay, eSewa and eSewa IME as one family of local wallets, not three competing products. Pick the one your top payment accepts, fund it cleanly, and keep the foreign-currency tools out of this job.

FAQ

Is IME Pay the same as eSewa?

No. They are related but distinct: eSewa and IME Pay are separate wallets with their own accounts, balances and acceptance. You can hold both, and they top up and settle independently.

Why would I use more than one wallet?

Because acceptance differs by merchant and by top-up option. If a merchant or a funding source only works on one, you hold the other for the rest. It costs nothing to keep both.

Are these wallets for receiving online sales?

Yes, for local customers. If you sell online in Nepal, accepting on the wallet your customers use is standard. The settlement and fee details vary, so check them before you build a store around them.

Sources and how to verify

Foreign-currency ceilings, card terms and bank fees are set by the NRB and each bank and change; confirm the current limit and fee with your bank before you rely on a number. General information, not financial advice.
