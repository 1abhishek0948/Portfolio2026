Website Security Checklist for Nepali Businesses

The security habits that keep a Nepali business website safe, in the order that matters: the cheap ones first, the credential ones, and the one that is the difference between a breach and a near miss.

Abhishek Thakur Published Oct 27, 2026 · Updated Oct 27, 2026· 3 min read

Most small sites are not broken by a clever attack; they are taken over by a login that was shared, or they stay broken because they stopped updating, or they lose everything because the backup was never tested. This checklist is the three, in the order that keeps the site safe, and they are the cheap ones, not the expensive tools.

This is a practical checklist, not a guarantee; the specifics fit your stack.

The three habits, in the order that matters

Habit What it is Why it is first
The update, done The software and the parts stay current The broken site is the one that stopped updating
The credential Fewer, stronger, separate logins The takeover is the login, not the break-in
The tested backup A copy that has been restored, not just made The difference between a breach and a loss

The order is the point, because the first two prevent the common ones and the third is what turns the rare one into a recovery instead of a loss, and the three are all cheap, which is why they are the ones that are skipped.

The update, the habit that is the whole defense

The broken site is the one that stopped updating, because the part that had the hole is the one that the update closes, and the site that is six updates behind is the site that has the hole that is known and patched. The habit is the update as a routine, not a project, and it is the one that prevents the breach ahead of every tool, and it is the reason the security is a habit, not a purchase.

The credential, where the cheap site is taken over

The attacker does not break the cheap site, they log into it, with the shared password, the admin that is the same as the content one, or the one that never changed. The move is the fewer, stronger, separate logins: one strong one for the person, the admin separate from the content, and the one that is not shared, because the shared login is the door that is open, and the one that is not the person's is the one that is not accountable.

The backup that is tested, not just made

The backup that has never been restored is not a backup, it is a hope, and the move is the one that has been put back, because the tested backup is the difference between the breach being a recovery and being a loss, and it is the cheap habit that is usually the missing one. See website maintenance and retainers in Nepal for the routine that carries the three, and the stack that has to be secure to begin with is the subject of choosing the right tech stack.

A sensible default

Make the update a routine, keep the credentials few, strong and separate, and test the backup so it is a recovery and not a hope. The three are cheap and they are the order, and doing them is the site that stays safe, which is the whole of the security for the small business, and the one that is not a purchase, it is a habit.

Bottom line: the small site is kept safe by three cheap habits in order: the update as a routine, the credential few and strong and separate, and the backup that has been tested. Skip the purchase and keep the habit, and the site is the one that is not the next broken one.

FAQ

What is the single most important security habit?

The update, done. The broken site is almost always the one that stopped updating, and the habit of the update is the one that prevents the breach, ahead of every tool you could buy.

What should I do about the logins?

Fewer, stronger, and separate. One login for the person, a strong one, not shared, and the admin login is not the same as the content login. The credential is where the cheap site is taken over, because the attacker does not break in, they log in.

What is the difference between a breach and a near miss?

The backup that is tested. A site with a working, tested backup meets a breach as a recovery, and a site without one meets it as a loss, and that one habit is the difference, and it is the cheap one that is usually missing.
