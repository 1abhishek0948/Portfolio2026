The tech stack is not the newest tool; it is the one that does the job, that the team can run, and that is cheap to change, and the three are the fit, and the newest is the one that fails them, and the boring one is the one that passes. This guide is the three, the boring answer, and the one that keeps the project alive after the launch.

This is judgment about your project; the specifics are the test, not a fact.

## The three questions, and the one that is the newest

| Question | The fit answer | The newest answer |
|---|---|---|
| The job it does | The one that is the known way to do it | The one that is the new way to do it, unproven for this |
| The team that runs it | The one the team knows | The one the team has to learn, now |
| The cost to change it | The one that is the cheap change | The one that is the expensive one, later |

The three are the fit, and the newest fails all three, and the boring one passes all three, and that is the answer, and the move is to ask the three, not to name the tool.

## The boring answer, and why it is the one

The boring answer is the one that is the known, the one the team can run, and the one that is the cheap change, and it is the boring one because it is not the newest, and it is the one that is the alive one, because the alive one is the one that is the known, and the known is the boring. The move is to the boring, and the boring is the one that is the three, and the three are the fit, and the fit is the alive one.
