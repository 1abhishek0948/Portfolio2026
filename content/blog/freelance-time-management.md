The reason freelancers miss the deadline or live in it is not effort; it is that the hours and the buffer are decided after the work has started, which means the deadline is always the last night. This guide is the two moves that put the decision before the work, and the one rule that keeps the client from ever having to chase.

This is judgment about your own schedule, not a fact.

## The two moves that come before the work

| Move | What it is | What it stops |
|---|---|---|
| The hour cap | A weekly number you will not exceed during a busy stretch | The deadline becoming all night |
| The known-unknown buffer | The extra time for revisions, waits and the local connectivity dip | The estimate being the optimistic one |

The cap is the move that protects the life, and the buffer is the move that protects the deadline, and both are decided before the work starts, which is the whole difference from the freelancer who decides both after.

## The buffer is not padding

The buffer is the part every estimate is wrong about: the revision round, the client who is slow to answer, the connection that dips on the delivery day. It is the known unknown, and it is the reason the first estimate is always the optimistic one. The habit is to add the buffer to the estimate and to tell the client the buffered number, not the optimistic one, because the buffered number is the one you can keep, and keeping it is the reputation.

## The one rule that keeps the client clean

State the hours and the deadline up front, and keep them. The client who is told the plan does not have to chase, and the freelancer who keeps the stated plan is the one the next client asks for. The rule is cheap and it compounds, because the asset of a freelancer is not the hour, it is the reputation for keeping the word, and that is built by the stated, kept plan, not by the hero last night. The communication side of it is in [client communication habits that build repeat business](/blog/client-communication-habits/).

## The local texture that the estimate must carry

The estimate is not just the work; it is the work through a connection that can dip and a time zone that can surprise. The buffer is where those two live, and the freelancer who prices them in and the one who does not are the same at the start of the week and very different at the deadline, because the second one is meeting it with the last night, which is the version that burns out.

## A sensible default

Cap the hours before the work, add the known-unknown buffer to the estimate and tell the client the buffered number, and state and keep the plan. Do that and the deadline is met by the plan, the income does not eat the life, and the reputation for keeping the word is the asset that brings the next client.

**Bottom line:** the deadline is won before the work, by the hour cap and the known-unknown buffer, and kept by stating and holding the plan. Decide the two before you start and keep the word you gave, and the last night stops being the whole strategy.
