Hiring a freelance web developer in Nepal is low-risk when it is done properly and a real gamble when it is not, and the difference is almost entirely in how you brief the job and set the terms. A clear, written, scoped brief and a small paid trial task will save you far more than any amount of interviewing. This guide is the process, from the brief to the terms to the mistakes that turn a hire into a mess.

Rates, availability and tools change, so confirm current specifics with each developer you are actually considering. This is practical guidance, not a recommendation of any person.

## The brief is the most important document

Most bad freelance outcomes start with a bad brief, not with a bad developer. A good brief is short and specific, and it answers:

- **What you want, in plain words**, the job, not the technology.
- **What it must do**, in a checkable list.
- **What it does not need**, which stops the scope from drifting.
- **The deadline and the budget band**, so both sides are in the same room.
- **What "done" means**, in a way you can point at and say yes.

A freelancer who can read that brief and agree to it is a better match than one who is fancier but vague. The brief is also your protection later, because it is what you judge delivery against.

## Vetting: the two tests that actually work

Resumes and portfolios lie by omission, but two tests do not.

1. **The live-work test.** Ask to see something they built that is still live, and what they changed after launch. A developer who can talk about what broke and what they fixed has real experience; one who only shows a mockup has not been tested.
2. **The paid trial task.** A small, scoped, paid task is the sharpest single signal. It shows how they build, how they communicate, and whether they hit the deadline. Pay it; the task is the interview, and a developer who will not do a paid trial is telling you something.

Neither test is perfect, but together they replace hours of small talk with evidence. For the broader buying side, the same instincts apply to a firm; see [how to choose a website development company in Nepal](/blog/website-development-company-nepal/).

## The terms that prevent the mess

Once you have a candidate, put the terms in writing. The short list:

- **The scope**, the deliverables, and the out-of-scope list, from the brief.
- **The price model**, fixed for a clear deliverable, or hourly for open work, and what moves it.
- **Ownership**, who holds the code, the domain and the hosting, and how they transfer.
- **Milestones and payments**, tied to delivered, accepted pieces, not to promises.
- **Support after launch**, a named window and scope, not "we are here."

The ownership line is the one that is most often missing and most regretted. A developer who holds your domain and hosting and is your only path to a change has turned a hire into a lock-in.

## The mistakes that turn a hire into a mess

| Mistake | The fix |
|---|---|
| Hiring on price alone | Hire on the brief and the trial, then price |
| No written scope | Write it; it is your protection |
| Paying everything up front | Tie payments to accepted milestones |
| No ownership terms | Agree who holds code, domain, hosting |
| Vague "done" | Define done in a checkable way |
| No plan for after launch | Agree a support window and scope |

Each of these is cheap to fix before you start and expensive to fix after.

## Individual vs firm, for your scope

The developer-vs-firm question is about your scope, not about which is "better."

- **A strong individual** is fast, direct and usually cheaper; ideal for a focused site or a well-scoped build where you can own the maintenance.
- **A firm** offers a team and a support desk; better when the work is ongoing or the scope is larger.

If you hire an individual, the maintenance question is on you, so plan for it. See [custom web application development in Nepal](/blog/custom-web-application-development-nepal/) if the scope is a real system, not a site.

## A short hiring checklist

- [ ] The brief is written: what, what it must do, what it does not, deadline, budget, "done"
- [ ] Two or three candidates shortlisted, each with live work to show
- [ ] A small paid trial task run, and the result judged
- [ ] Terms in writing: scope, price model, ownership, milestones, support
- [ ] A plan for maintenance and for what happens after the developer is gone

Run that checklist and the hire is a decision on evidence; skip it and the hire is a coin flip with a price tag.

**Bottom line:** the safest freelance developer in Nepal is the one you choose on a written brief, a live-work check and a paid trial task, with terms that name the owner of the code, the domain and the hosting. Do the boring paper and the boring trial, and the hire stops being a gamble and becomes a process.
