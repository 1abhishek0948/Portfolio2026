PrabhuPay matters to a Nepali business for one reason: it is tied to the bank side, not to a stand-alone consumer app. If your money already lives in the banking system, that is a clean place to run everyday payments and collections. This guide is the setup-and-use map, and where it fits against the stand-alone wallets.

Bank terms, available products and fees are set by the institution and change, so confirm the current position with your branch. This is general guidance, not financial advice.

## Why the bank side matters

A stand-alone wallet is fast and consumer-friendly, but it is one more system your money passes through. A bank-linked option keeps more of the flow inside the banking relationship you already have, which usually means:

- Collections settle where you already bank.
- The setup is a branch conversation, not a self-serve sign-up.
- The terms are bank terms, with a fee schedule you can read and confirm.

That "confirm the fee schedule" habit is the whole value of using it: you are not guessing.

## Setup, in practice

The setup is short, and most of it is a branch interaction:

1. **Confirm the product exists** for your account type, and what it covers (payments, collections, both).
2. **Agree the fees and limits** in writing or on paper, because these are the numbers that decide whether it is worth it.
3. **Wire it to how you actually move money**, so the daily flow is one action, not three.
4. **Test with a small real transaction** before you rely on it for a bigger number.

For the developer angle of accepting these payments on a site, see [setting up eSewa and Khalti payment gateway APIs](/blog/payment-gateway-setup/).

## Where it fits vs the stand-alone wallets

| If you want | The better fit is |
|---|---|
| Payments to sit with your banking | PrabhuPay |
| A fast consumer top-up habit | eSewa or Khalti |
| Developer collections on a store | A gateway API (eSewa/Khalti/IME Pay) |
| A foreign payment | A card or cross-border method, not this |

The last row is the one people get wrong: a bank-linked Nepali service is not how you pay a foreign vendor.

## A sensible default

If you bank at the institution behind PrabhuPay and want collections and everyday payments to stay inside that relationship, set it up with your branch, read the fee schedule, and test a small transaction. If your main need is a fast consumer top-up, keep eSewa or Khalti for that and let the bank side handle the collections.

**Bottom line:** PrabhuPay earns its place when you want money to move through your existing banking rather than another app. Confirm the products and fees with your branch, test with a small number, and keep the foreign-currency job off this tool.
