The reason beginners lose money in NEPSE is almost never the stock they bought. It is the unprepared start: no clean record, no idea what the fees are, no plan for when to sell, and a size of money bigger than their patience. This guide is the calm version of the fundamentals, and the mistakes that turn the fundamentals into a loss.

Market conditions, broker fees and rules change, so confirm the current ones. This is general information, not financial advice, and not a recommendation of any stock.

## What the market actually is

NEPSE is Nepal's stock exchange: you are buying a small, transferable piece of a company, held electronically in your demat account against your MeroShare number, through a broker. That is the whole machine. Everything else, the apps, the tips, the mood, is decoration around those four things.

## The order to learn it in

Do not learn it in the order the tips arrive; learn it in the order the money moves:

1. **The plumbing first.** MeroShare, demat, a broker, funded. See [how to set up a MeroShare and demat account](/blog/mero-share-account/).
2. **The cost.** Broker fees, any transaction cost, and the time it takes to settle, so you know what "one buy" actually costs.
3. **The record.** One dated list: what you bought, for how much, through whom. This is what you will need for tax and for every later decision.
4. **The first buy, small.** A simple order, watched all the way through, so the chain is proven before it matters.

## The four mistakes beginners make

| Mistake | The fix |
|---|---|
| Starting with more money than their patience | Start small enough to hold through the noise |
| No written reason for the buy | Write the reason and the sell condition together |
| No record | One dated list, updated every time |
| Deciding to sell on a tip | The sell is the same decision you wrote at the buy |

The last one is the one that turns an investor into a gambler, and it is avoidable with one habit: the sell condition is written at the moment of the buy, not at the moment of the fear.

## The number to check before anything else

Before you pick a company, check whether you can get out on purpose, and what it costs. If the answer is unclear, the size of the position is wrong. A small, trackable, exitable position is the whole beginner's goal.

## A sensible default

Set up the plumbing, fund a little, record every rupee, write the sell condition at the buy, and keep the position small enough that a bad year is a lesson, not a disaster. That is the entire beginner's skill set.

**Bottom line:** the fundamentals of NEPSE are the plumbing, the cost, the record and the exit plan, in that order. Do the plumbing and the record before the picks, keep the size small, and write the sell at the buy, and you will be in front of most beginners who skipped all four.
