The .com.np domain is more than a purchase; it is a record, and the record is the thing you hold, not the login to the shop that sold it. This guide is the process, the documents, and the two details that decide whether the name is actually yours and stays yours.

Registry rules and the document list change; confirm the current ones with the registry or your provider. This is a process guide, not legal advice.

## The two details that matter more than the price

| Detail | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| The record you hold | The registration is a real record in your name, not just a shop login | The record is what you can prove is yours |
| The DNS and transfer in your control | You can point the name and move it without the provider's permission | The one that turns the domain into a possession instead of a rental |

The second is the one that decides the whole question, because a domain whose DNS and transfer are locked behind a provider is a rental, and the exit is the moment the provider says no, and that is the version you cannot get back.

## The process, in order

1. **Check the name is free** and the form you want, .com.np or .np, in one move.
2. **Get the document list** for your type, a business or a person, from the registry or the provider, because the list is what stalls the application.
3. **Register, and take the record into your own control**, the account that is yours, not the provider's.
4. **Hold the DNS and the transfer ability**, so the name is portable, and renew on your own record, not a reminder the provider sends.

Step four is the habit, and it is the one that keeps the domain a possession, because the record and the control together are the thing that is yours, and either one missing is the version that is not.

## Why the local name still matters

The .com.np is the local signal, and it is the one that says the site is of Nepal, and for a local business that is a real value, and it is the one that pairs with the local hosting and the local payment, which is the whole "local" stack. ## A sensible default

Check the name, get the document list for your type, register into an account you control, and hold the DNS and the transfer so the name is portable. Do that and the .com.np is a record you own, which is the only version of it that is actually yours, and the one that stays yours.

**Bottom line:** the .com.np is a record, and the two details that make it yours are the record in your name and the DNS and transfer in your control. Get the document list, register into your own account, and hold the control, and the name is a possession, not a rental the provider can keep.
