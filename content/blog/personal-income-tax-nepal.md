Income tax in Nepal is not the thing that surprises people; the surprise is not knowing the current rule when the moment arrives. The rates, the thresholds and the deductions are set by the tax authority and they move, so the honest version of this guide is not a table of numbers to memorise. It is the map of who is taxed, what counts, how filing works, and the one record that makes all of it a lookup instead of a scramble.

This is general information, not tax advice. For your own numbers, use a current official source or a tax professional.

## Who is actually in scope

Three groups, roughly, and your position in one of them decides most of what follows:

- **Salaried**, where much of the tax is handled at the source and your job is mostly to check it.
- **Business or professional income**, where you track it yourself and it is yours to file.
- **Investment and rental income**, which often gets overlooked by people who assume "I am not an employer, so no."

If your money comes from more than one of these, that is when the record becomes the difference between a calm filing and a bad one.

## What counts as income

Keep it simple and complete: salary, business, freelance or professional fees, rent, and investment income like dividends and gains. The detail that matters is not the total; it is that each is dated and sourced, so when you need to answer "where did this come from" you can. For the freelance slice specifically, see [freelance income tax in Nepal](/blog/freelance-income-tax-nepal/).

## How filing actually works

The shape is the same for most people:

1. **Know the period and the threshold** for your situation, from a current official source, not a memory.
2. **Gather the record**, one dated list of income by type.
3. **Check what is handled at source** (salary often is) and what is not.
4. **File on time and keep the acknowledgement**, and note the next due date so it is a repeat, not a crisis.

That fourth step is the whole skill: turn the annual scramble into a two-line reminder.

## The one habit that keeps you clean

A dated, sourced income record. Everything else in tax, the rates, the deductions, the thresholds, is a value that changes and that you look up. The record does not change, and it is the thing you can be asked to produce. Keep it and the system is honest; drop it and every year you are rebuilding from memory.

## A sensible default

Put a reminder on the calendar for the filing window, keep one dated income list by type, confirm the current threshold at the moment it matters, and keep every acknowledgement. Do that and you are in the "lookup, not reconstruction" camp, which is the entire goal.

**Bottom line:** personal income tax in Nepal is about who is in scope, what counts, and a filed record. The numbers move, so confirm them at the moment they matter; the record does not move, so build it every year. Those two habits are the whole game.
