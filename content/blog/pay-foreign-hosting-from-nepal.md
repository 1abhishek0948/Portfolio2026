Paying for foreign web hosting from Nepal trips up a lot of developers. You have a client project, or a side project, or a domain that expires, and the invoice is in US dollars from a host that does not accept eSewa or Khalti. This guide lays out the methods that actually clear for Nepali buyers, in the order I would try them, so you can pick the one that fits your situation.

Two grounding notes: exact fees, limits and what each provider accepts change, and international card rules sit under NRB regulation. Confirm current details with your bank and the provider. This is practical guidance, not financial advice.

## The methods, in the order to try them

| Method | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| International card (Visa/Mastercard) on a local account | Small, occasional bills | Conversion spread on every charge |
| Dollar / USD card | Frequent foreign spend, avoiding repeated conversion | Needs a documented foreign-currency source |
| Payoneer balance | Paying providers that accept Payoneer | Top-up path and fees into your account |
| Wise account | Frequent, larger cross-border payments | Availability and what the provider accepts |
| Friend / team member's card | One-off emergencies | Trust, and the owner bears the charge |

For most people starting out, the answer is simply: **an international card**. It is the cheapest to set up and clears at almost every host. You only graduate to a dollar card or a cross-border wallet when the conversion spread on repeated charges becomes annoying enough to bother fixing.

## Method 1: the international card (start here)

If you have a card that is enabled for **online and international** transactions, you can usually pay for hosting directly. The whole setup is:

1. Confirm online / e-commerce transactions are on (this is the most common reason for a decline).
2. Confirm the card's monthly or per-transaction limit clears the bill.
3. Pay, and note that the amount is converted at the bank's rate.

For a one or two a year hosting bill, that conversion spread is a few dollars. Ignore it and move on. This is the path if you are not moving foreign money often.

## Method 2: a dollar card (for frequent foreign spend)

If you pay for hosting regularly, or your business has ongoing foreign costs, a USD card lets you spend from a dollar balance and avoid converting rupees over and over. The catch is the paperwork: you need a USD account with a clean, documented source, and NRB rules cap how much you can hold and spend.

Work through [how to get a dollar card in Nepal](/blog/how-to-get-dollar-card-nepal/) and confirm the current [NRB dollar card limits](/blog/nrb-dollar-card-rules-nepal/) before relying on it for a big renewals bill.

## Method 3: cross-border wallets (Payoneer, Wise)

Some providers accept cross-border wallet payments. For freelancers, a Payoneer account that already holds your client money can often pay a provider that accepts Payoneer, which keeps everything in one foreign-currency flow. Wise is similar for frequent, larger payments.

The honest limit: these wallets only help where the *provider* accepts them. If your host only takes cards, the wallet money has to move to a card first, which brings back the fees. So check the provider's accepted methods before you build a workflow around a wallet. The full picture is in [PayPal alternatives in Nepal](/blog/paypal-alternatives-nepal/).

## The common declines, and the one-line fix

Almost every "I cannot pay my host from Nepal" story is one of these:

- **Declined for being international.** Fix: turn on international / online transactions.
- **Declined for the limit.** Fix: raise the limit or split the payment.
- **Currency routing surprise.** Fix: expect the conversion spread, batch your spend.
- **3-D Secure / verification loop.** Fix: use the bank's verification, not the provider's, and keep your phone reachable.

## A simple decision for you

- One-off, small, rare bill: **international card**, done.
- Frequent foreign bills: **dollar card**, after you confirm NRB limits.
- You already hold foreign earnings in a wallet: **Pay the provider from that wallet** where accepted.
- Emergency, today: **a trusted team member's card**, settled back to them.

For the bigger picture of getting your money in and out of Nepal as a freelancer, read [how freelancers in Nepal get paid](/blog/how-freelancers-nepal-get-paid/).

**Bottom line:** most Nepali developers do not need a dollar card to pay for hosting. They need a normal international card with online transactions enabled and a limit that clears the bill. Add a dollar card or a cross-border wallet only when repeated conversion starts to cost you real money, and confirm the NRB ceilings first.
