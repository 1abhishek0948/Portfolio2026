The costliest freelance losses are not to the obvious bad actors; they are to the client who looked fine, the deal that looked bigger, and the shape that only shows itself when the work is done and the payment is not. This guide is the patterns to read before you start and the two habits that keep the risk where it belongs, on the deal, not on you.

This is risk judgment, not a fact; the patterns are general, and your read of a specific deal is yours.

## The patterns that show up early

| Pattern | What it looks like | The check |
|---|---|---|
| No path to payment | A big ask, a small deposit, the rest at the end | Does the payment reach you, on a schedule, before the end? |
| Off the agreed path | "Let us sort payment later" or "we will pay when it is live" | The payment is agreed, in writing, on a schedule, before the work |
| The scope that grows free | The job is small when you start and large when you are in it | A named out-of-scope list, so the growth is a new price |
| The credential you cannot verify | A company that is impressive and uncheckable | You can reach a real person and a real payment, or it is a story |

The point of the table is that the risk is in the shape, and the shape is readable before you invest, which is the whole reason to read it before you start.

## The one red flag that overrides the rest

The client moving the work off the agreed payment path is the flag that overrides how nice the client is. A good client on the agreed path is a good client; the same client who says the payment will be sorted later, or who pulls the work off the platform, has just moved the risk entirely onto you. That move is the one to not accept, and it is the one the deposit and the written schedule exist to stop, which is the ground covered in [freelance contracts and payment terms](/blog/freelance-contracts-nepal/).

## The two habits that keep you clean

One, the deposit before the risk: a portion on the start, tied to the agreement, so the work does not begin with the full risk on you. Two, the payment path that actually reaches you: a confirmed way the money gets to your account, because a payment you cannot collect is not a payment, and that path is [how freelancers in Nepal get paid](/blog/how-freelancers-nepal-get-paid/). The habits are small, and they are the difference between a deal and a trap.

## The good-looking deal, checked hardest

The instinct is to relax when the client is impressive, and that is exactly when the size makes the risk bigger, not smaller. The habits are the same on the big deal as on the small one, because the habit is the protection, not the amount. A big deal with the deposit, the written scope and the confirmed payment path is a good deal; the same big deal without them is the expensive one.

## A sensible default

Read the shape before the work: the payment path, the deposit, the out-of-scope list, and the verifiable person. If the deal moves the work off the agreed path, walk. Do that and the scams you meet are the ones you recognized early, which is the only version that costs you nothing.

**Bottom line:** the risk of a freelance deal is in its shape, and the shape is readable before you start. Check the payment path, take the deposit, name the out-of-scope, and treat the move off the agreed path as the flag that overrides the rest, and the scams stay the ones you did not fall for.
