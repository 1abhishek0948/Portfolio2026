The single most useful thing about NRB foreign-currency rules is not a number, because the numbers move. It is knowing the *shape* of the rules, and the two habits that keep you clean under any version of the numbers. This guide is the shape, and where to confirm the numbers when it matters.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice. Confirm the current rules with your bank or the regulator.

## The shape, not the numbers

The rules govern three things, and all of your foreign-currency questions fit inside them:

1. **How much** a resident or business can hold and move.
2. **How it got here**, the source, which must be clean and documented.
3. **Through which channels**, the banks and the approved rails.

Your individual bank implements the rules and adds its own limits on top, so the real ceiling on any move is the regulator's limit *and* the bank's, whichever is lower. That is why the answer always comes from the desk, not from a page.

## The two habits that keep it clean

Everything else is detail. These two carry the rest:

- **Document the source.** Whatever the flow, the clean version is the one where you can show how it arrived, dated. The same habit that makes a dollar card easy makes every other foreign-currency move easy.
- **Confirm the number, dated.** Ask the desk for the current ceiling and the channel for your case, and write it down with the date, because the next year's answer is not this year's.

The source habit is why a documented freelancer or exporter moves money smoothly and an unclear one stalls. See [NRB dollar card rules and limits](/blog/nrb-dollar-card-rules-nepal/) for how the shape looks specifically on a card, and [cross-border payments from Nepal](/blog/cross-border-payments-nepal/) for the paying side.

## What changes, and what does not

The numbers change, the channels change, the paperwork changes. The two habits do not, and they are the part you control. A person who keeps the source documented and confirms the number when it matters is clean under any version of the rules, and a person who does not is not clean under any of them.

## A sensible default

Treat the rules as a shape, not a figure. Document the source of any foreign money, confirm the current ceiling and channel at the desk with a date on the answer, and let the bank's limit and the regulator's limit both bound your move. Do that and the rules are a background you never have to argue with.

**Bottom line:** NRB foreign-currency rules are about the amount, the source and the channel, and the numbers move. Keep the source documented and confirm the current number at the desk, dated, and you are clean under every version of the rules that comes.
