A digital marketing agency in Nepal can multiply your reach, but only if you understand what an agency actually does and what it cannot do. The agencies that disappoint are usually not the bad ones; they are the ones hired for the wrong job, or the ones that run ads while calling it strategy. This guide is the "what they do, when you need one, and how to pick the right kind" half of the decision.

Fees, platform terms and what each channel supports change, so confirm current specifics with the agency you are considering. This is a framework, not a recommendation of any firm.

## What an agency actually does

Strip the jargon and an agency does four jobs. It gets you found, it turns interest into a customer, it measures whether the money is working, and it keeps the whole thing running. The good ones do all four; the ones that disappoint usually do only one.

| Job | What it looks like | The signal that it is done well |
|---|---|---|
| Get you found | Search, social, ads, content | You get consistent, trackable interest, not a spike |
| Turn interest into a customer | A page, a flow, a follow-up | A customer arrives with a known source |
| Measure the money | Track cost per customer, not just clicks | You can say what one customer costs |
| Keep it running | A system, not a project | Results hold when the campaign pauses |

If an agency only does the first job and calls it marketing, that is why the results stop when the ads stop.

## When you actually need one

An agency is a multiplier, and a multiplier on a weak base is still weak. The honest checks:

- **Can you serve more demand?** If your product, delivery or service will buckle under more customers, do not buy reach yet.
- **Do you have a base that works?** If you cannot get a customer on your own, an agency will not; it scales what already works.
- **Do you have time to run it yourself?** If one or two channels done well fit your time, start there and add the agency when the mix outgrows you.

An agency earns its fee when it costs less than the customers it brings in and you can serve them. That is the whole business case, in one line. For what that looks like in numbers, read [digital marketing services price in Nepal](/blog/digital-marketing-services-price-nepal/).

## The "just runs ads" test

The fastest way to separate an agency that runs ads from one that does marketing is to ask three questions and read the answer:

1. **What happens after the ad stops?** A strategy answer mentions the asset you built (the page, the list, the search position), not "we start again."
2. **What is a customer worth to you, and how do you measure it?** An agency that tracks a customer, not a click, is doing marketing.
3. **What is the plan beyond this campaign?** A real agency has a sequence, not a single push.

If all three answers are "we run the ads and report impressions," you have found an ad runner. That is not always wrong, but it is not the job you think you are buying.

## What to look for, specifically

- **A named owner** for your account, not a rotating handoff.
- **The right channel mix** for your market, not the agency's favorite.
- **Measurement you can read**, tied to a customer, with a cost per customer.
- **A written scope** of what is included, what is not, and what "done" or "review" means each period.
- **Honesty about ramp-up**, because a channel takes time to build, and the agencies that promise instant results are the ones selling a spike.

## A simple hiring move

1. **Write your one-page need**: the product, the customer, the goal, the budget, and what "working" means in a number.
2. **Shortlist two or three** agencies who show the four jobs, not just the ads.
3. **Run the three questions** and read the answers, not the enthusiasm.
4. **Ask for a written scope** and a customer-level metric.
5. **Pick the one that measures a customer** and names the owner.

The one-page need you write is the document you will judge them against, and it is worth more than any agency you hire.

**Bottom line:** an agency in Nepal multiplies reach, measurement and a running system, but it cannot fix a base you cannot serve. Pick the one that does all four jobs, that measures a customer rather than a click, and that will tell you what happens after the ad stops. Do that and the agency is an asset; skip it and it is a more expensive way to run ads.
