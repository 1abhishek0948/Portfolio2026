Before you can buy a single share of anything on NEPSE, you need two things to exist: your MeroShare investor number and a demat account that actually holds the shares. This guide is the setup map, and it is the foundation for everything else in investing.

Rules, document lists and how much is online vs offline change, so confirm the current process with your broker. This is general information, not financial advice.

## The two pieces, and why both

- **MeroShare** is your central investor record: one number, one name, across your holdings. It is how the market knows the shares are yours.
- **Demat** is the account where shares live electronically, linked to that number and opened through a broker.

You need both, and the demat account is the one you interact with daily; MeroShare is the identity underneath it.

## The setup, in order

1. **Pick a broker.** This is the front door; the opening happens through them, and their fees and service decide a lot of your experience.
2. **Complete the forms and documents.** Identity, PAN, photo, and the broker's opening forms, the exact set of which you should get in writing from them first.
3. **Get your MeroShare number** linked to the account.
4. **Fund the demat account** and make one small test buy, so you confirm the whole chain works before real money moves.

That test transaction is the step people skip and then regret, because it is the only moment you find out whether the record is clean.

## The habit that keeps it clean

Treat your record like the one that matters: keep the MeroShare number, the broker details, and every purchase on one dated list. That single habit is what makes tax time, transfers and any later move from a headache into a copy-paste. See [personal income tax in Nepal](/blog/personal-income-tax-nepal/) for why the record is worth keeping.

## A sensible default

Open through one broker you will actually keep, get the MeroShare number linked, fund a little, and test one buy. Do not scatter across brokers before the first one is clean, because that is where records get messy.

**Bottom line:** MeroShare is your identity as an investor, and demat is where your shares live; you need both, opened through a broker. Get the document list in writing, test a small buy, and keep the record in one place, and the whole system stays clean.
