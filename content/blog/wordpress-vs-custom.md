The WordPress-or-custom question is not about which is better; it is about the gap between what you need and what the CMS gives you, and the gap is the only thing that should decide it. This guide is the question, the cost on each side, and the move that keeps you from paying custom for a small gap.

This is judgment about your business, not a fact.

## The question that decides it

| If the gap is... | The answer is... |
|---|---|
| Small, and the CMS does 80% well | The CMS, because the gap is not worth the build |
| Large, and the process is genuinely yours | Custom, because the workaround costs more than the build |
| Unknown, because you have not tried the CMS | The CMS first, because the gap is a guess until you have it |

The last row is the most common mistake, and it is building custom on a guess, which is the most expensive version of the whole decision, because the gap was a hope, not a measured thing.

## The cost on each side, the whole life

The CMS is the cheaper door and the recurring bill: the updates, the plugin, the theme that changes, and the small, unglamorous cost that follows the launch. Custom is the expensive door and the owned one: the build is the big number, and after it the cost is the maintenance of something you own, which is cheaper per year than the CMS recurring one, if the gap was real. The decision is the whole life, not the launch, and the launch is where most people price it, which is the version that misses the recurring CMS bill and the custom ownership.

## Why the small gap is the trap

The instinct is to build custom to avoid a small CMS friction, and that is the trap, because the friction is worth less than the build, and the custom project that is built to avoid a small gap is the slow, expensive one with no user in the middle. The move is to take the CMS through the gap first, and only build the custom piece where the gap is the whole process, and that is the shape of [custom web application development in Nepal](/blog/custom-web-application-development-nepal/).

## The buying side of it

Whichever you pick, the builder is the thing to choose well, and the CMS does not excuse a bad builder and custom does not rescue one. The instinct for the choice is the same as for any build, and it is covered in [how to choose a website development company in Nepal](/blog/website-development-company-nepal/).

## A sensible default

Measure the gap by taking the CMS through it, and only build custom where the gap is the whole process, not a friction. Price the whole life, not the launch, and keep the builder the deciding factor. Do that and the tool is the one the gap earned, which is the only version that is not a guess or a trap.

**Bottom line:** the WordPress-or-custom answer is the size of the gap, not the prestige of the tool. Take the CMS through a small gap, build custom only for the whole process, price the whole life, and the tool is the one the decision earned, not the one the habit wanted.
